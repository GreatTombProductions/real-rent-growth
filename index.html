What this tool does

When the news says "rents are up 25%," that number is usually nominal — the sticker price. But the dollar itself shrank: over the same period, inflation raised prices across the economy. A county whose rents rose 10% while inflation was 10.5% actually became cheaper in real terms. This tool shows both numbers for every US county.

The data

  • Rents: median gross rent (Census table B25064) from the American Community Survey 5-year estimates, three vintages:
    • 2014 5-year (2010–2014) — pre-pre-pandemic baseline
    • 2019 5-year (2015–2019) — pre-pandemic baseline
    • 2023 5-year (2019–2023) — the current window
  • Inflation: Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), from the BLS via FRED. Annual averages are taken at each window's midpoint (2012, 2017, 2021) and the growth factor is applied multiplicatively — the same method EQR's institutional pipeline uses.

How to read the numbers

Nominal growth = the sticker change in median gross rent between two ACS windows. Real growth = nominal growth divided by the CPI factor for the same period, minus one. If real growth is negative, rents rose slower than inflation — renters' purchasing power in that county improved relative to prices elsewhere.

Example: Jefferson County, Alabama. Median gross rent went $811 (2010–2014) → $900 (2015–2019) → $1,132 (2019–2023). Over the recent window that's +25.8% nominal, but inflation was +10.6% — the real increase is +13.8%.

Caveats

  • These are medians of different renter populations, not a repeat-rent index tracking the same units. Growth is the ratio of vintage medians; actual renters' experience varies with unit turnover and quality mix.
  • ACS 5-year estimates carry margins of error, not shown here.
  • Counties with no cash-rent households or suppressed values are omitted from the affected window.
  • Median gross rent includes utilities paid by the renter. CPI-U is national, not county-level; local inflation differs.

Sources

  • BLS Consumer Price Index (CPI-U, series CPIAUCSL) — fred.stlouisfed.org
